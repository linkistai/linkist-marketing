Most professionals already have two tools for people. The phone holds a list of names and numbers. The company, if it sells anything, holds a CRM with deals moving through stages. Neither answers the question that actually costs you opportunities: who did I meet, why did they matter, and what should I do about it this week.

A Personal Relationship Manager, a PRM, is built for that question. This article draws the line between the three tools, so you know what you are getting and what you are not.

## The contacts app: people without context

A contacts app stores a person. Name, number, email, perhaps a company. It is excellent at one thing, which is being reachable from any device. It is silent on everything that makes a relationship useful: where you met, what you talked about, what you promised, and when you last spoke.

That silence is why a contact list grows and a network does not. After a busy month the list has 40 new entries and the memory of most of them has already faded.

## The CRM: deals without people

A CRM is organised around the deal. Leads become opportunities, opportunities move through stages, stages have values, and the pipeline report goes to a manager on Monday. That is the right shape for a sales team and the wrong shape for a person.

Most of the relationships that matter to a professional are not deals. A former colleague, an investor you met once, the person who introduced you to your best client. A CRM has nowhere sensible to put them, so they do not go in, and the tool ends up describing a tenth of the network.

## The PRM: people, context, next action

A PRM starts from the person and keeps three things with them.

- **Context.** Where you met, what mattered, tags, a voice note recorded while it was fresh, the history of every interaction.
- **Fit.** Who among your contacts matches what you are looking for now, and which relationships are warming up or going quiet.
- **The next action.** What deserves attention today, before the moment passes.

Linkist arranges this as three stages: Capture and share, Build relationships, Act and grow. Capture gets the person in with their context. Build finds who matters, with Natural-Language Search, ICP Matching, Relationship Priority and Network Ask. Act tells you what to do next, with Top Actions, the Weekly Planner, Intelligent Nudges and Warm Introductions.

::note
A useful test: if a tool can tell you who to follow up with this week and why, it is doing PRM work. If it can only tell you who someone is, it is a contacts app. If it can only tell you what a deal is worth, it is a CRM.
::

## What a PRM is not

It is not a replacement for the phone book. Linkist imports your phone contacts, a CSV or a VCF file, and leaves them where they are. It is not a sales pipeline; Pro includes a small deal-tracking view for the people who want it, but the product is not organised around it. And it is not a social network. Nothing you save is published anywhere; your profile is the only public part, and you decide what it shows.
