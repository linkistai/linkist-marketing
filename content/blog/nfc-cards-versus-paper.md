The case against paper business cards is usually made with statistics about how many end up in a bin. Linkist does not use those numbers, because nobody can source them. The case does not need them. It rests on what a paper card cannot do after the handshake, and on what a tap does instead.

## What a paper card does

It transfers a set of details, once, in the state they were in on the day of printing. If you change roles, the cards in a hundred wallets are wrong. If the other person loses the card, the connection is gone. And it tells you nothing: you do not know whether it was kept, and you have nothing to remember the meeting by except your own memory.

## What a tap does

A Linkist NFC card carries a chip that opens your live profile on the other person's phone. Three things follow.

- **The details are current.** The card points at the profile, not at a snapshot, so a new role or number shows on the next tap. Nothing is reprinted.
- **They keep everything, in one place.** Your photo, title, company, links and whatever else the profile shows, saved to their phone from the browser. No app is needed on their side.
- **You keep the meeting.** If they use Linkist too, the exchange lands in your contacts with the context of where and when. If they do not, you capture them in 10 seconds with a tap, a scan or a note.

That third point is the one that matters for relationships. Paper ends at the exchange. A tap starts the record that the follow-up will need.

## Where paper still wins, and what to do about it

Not every phone has NFC switched on, and the terms are honest about it: NFC depends on compatible devices, settings and correct use, and no device is guaranteed. That is why the profile also lives behind a QR code and a link. Show the code when the tap does not take, or send the link after the meeting. Some people will still want a paper card to hold; giving them one costs nothing, as long as the record of the meeting goes into Linkist.

## The part that is not about the card

The card is the fastest way to start a connection. It is not what keeps one. What keeps it is the context saved with the person and the follow-up that happens on time. Every Linkist card includes PRM Essential for that reason: the tap opens the profile, and the PRM does the remembering.
