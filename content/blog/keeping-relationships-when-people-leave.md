Every company knows the moment. A good salesperson, partnerships lead or founder-adjacent fixer hands in their notice, and within a week the question arrives: who did they actually know, and what was going on with each of them. The CRM has the deals. The relationships, the context and the history are in a personal phone that is no longer in the building.

## Why the CRM does not solve it

A CRM records deals, and a deal is a small window into a relationship. The years of coffees before the deal, the person who made the introduction, the note that this buyer hates being chased on Fridays: none of that has a field. So it stays in heads and phones, and it leaves with them.

## Personal and shared, kept apart

The Team plan in Linkist draws one line and keeps to it. Every contact is either personal or shared with the team.

- **Personal contacts stay with the person.** A user's own network is theirs, and the company does not see it.
- **Shared contacts stay with the company.** Anything shared with the team, together with its relationship history, remains with the authorised team when the person leaves.

That line is what makes people willing to share. Nobody hands over a network wholesale; everybody can share the relationships that belong to the work.

::note
Sharing is per contact. The choice sits with the person who captured the relationship, and the history of a shared contact stays readable to the team afterwards.
::

## What the team gets

The Team plan gives every user everything in Pro, and adds four things for the company.

- Contact sharing across the team, per contact.
- A centralised admin console for members and shared contacts.
- Company branding on every card, so the profile a client saves carries the company as well as the person.
- A team directory.

The minimum is 5 users, billed per user monthly or yearly; the [pricing page](/pricing) shows the figures.

## Setting it up so the history is there when you need it

The plan only protects what was shared while the person was still in the building, so the habit matters more than the licence.

1. Agree what counts as a work relationship. Clients, partners, suppliers and prospects are the usual list.
2. Ask people to share at capture, not at exit. A contact tapped at a trade show for the company is shared the same day.
3. Keep the context flowing. Notes, voice notes and tags on shared contacts are what a successor will read; a name without context is only slightly better than nothing.
4. Use the directory. When someone new needs a supplier the company already knows, the directory answers before the request goes round by email.

## What to say to the team

Say the true thing: your network is yours, the company's relationships are the company's, and the tool keeps the two apart. People share more when they trust the line than when they are told to.
