There are two ways to reach someone you do not know. Write to them cold and compete with everything else in their inbox, or arrive through someone they already trust. The second way works more often, and it works because the introduction carries the connector's credibility, not yours. The catch is that most people cannot see the path. This playbook is about finding it and using it well.

## Step 1: say what you need, precisely

"An introduction to someone in fintech" is not a request; it is a category. "An introduction to a payments founder in Dubai who has raised a Series A and is hiring a head of partnerships" is a request. The precision does two things: it lets a connector picture the exact person, and it lets Linkist find the path.

In Linkist that request is a Network Ask. You post what you need, and Linkist finds relevant people in your network, or a trusted path to someone one connection away.

## Step 2: find the path, not just the person

The person you need is rarely in your contacts. The person who knows them often is. Network Ask and Warm Introductions look for that second person: someone you have context with, who has a relationship with the person you want to reach.

Before you ask, read the context you saved on the connector. Where you met, what you last talked about, what you did for them. If you have nothing saved, that is the first sign the introduction is premature.

::flow
Post what you need
See the path through your network
Read the context on the connector
Ask them, with the message written
Record the outcome
::

## Step 3: ask so that saying yes is easy

The connector should not have to think. Give them three things in one short message.

- **Who and why.** The person you want to reach and the specific reason, in 2 sentences.
- **A forwardable paragraph.** Written as if to the target, so the connector can send it on without editing. AI Follow-up can draft it from the context you saved; you edit and send.
- **An easy no.** "If this is not a good moment, no need to reply." People say yes more when no is free.

## Step 4: close the loop, twice

When the introduction happens, thank the connector the same day. When something comes of it, tell them. This is the part everyone skips and the part that makes the next introduction easy. Save both moments as interactions on the connector's record, so the history is there the next time you look at them.

## Step 5: keep the connector warm

Warm introductions come from warm relationships. Relationship Priority shows which relationships are warming up, cooling down or cold; a connector who has gone quiet for 6 months is not someone to ask for a favour this week. Intelligent Nudges prompt you before that happens, and the Weekly Planner gives the reconnect a slot.

## What Linkist does and does not do here

It finds the path, drafts the message and reminds you. It does not send anything, and it does not contact the connector or the target on your behalf. The ask is yours, in your words, from your account.
