Every professional carries an ideal customer profile in their head. The founder at the right stage, the buyer with the right title in the right sector, the partner in the right city. The trouble is that the network grows faster than the memory of who fits it. ICP Matching moves the profile out of your head and compares it with everyone you have saved.

## What an ICP is

ICP stands for ideal customer profile, and the word customer is looser than it sounds. An ICP can describe a customer, an investor, a hire, a partner or a speaker. It is a short description of who you are looking for: sector, role, company stage, location, the kind of problem they have.

You can keep more than one. A founder might hold one ICP for customers and another for angel investors; a recruiter might hold one per open role.

## What matching reads

ICP Matching works from what is in Linkist about each contact: profile data, contact details, your notes, connection history, the context of how you met, and professional information. Where you have enabled AI Enrichment, it also draws on public or professional sources to fill gaps in a record, which usually improves the match.

It does not read anything outside Linkist about you, and it does not read your contacts' private data. The privacy policy lists exactly what the AI features use, and the [AI and your data](/ai) page repeats it with the section cited.

## What it shows

Three things.

- **Who fits.** The contacts that match a given ICP, so the next outreach starts from the right list rather than the whole list.
- **How many.** The home screen shows the number of matches found for your ICPs, so you notice when yesterday's captures included the right people.
- **What to do.** Matches feed Top Actions and the Weekly Planner, so a new match becomes a follow-up rather than a statistic.

::flow
Describe who you need
Linkist compares it with your contacts
Matches appear on the home screen
Top Actions turns them into follow-ups
::

## Writing an ICP that matches well

Be concrete. "Fintech" matches more usefully than "finance". "Series A" is better than "growing". A city is better than a region. If a sentence would not help a colleague pick the right person out of your contacts, it will not help Linkist either.

Keep the context flowing in. Matching is only as good as the record. A tag for the event, a note about what the person needs and a voice note about what they said all give the match something to work with.

## What it cannot do

ICP Matching is assistive. It suggests, and you decide. The privacy policy says Linkist does not use AI for legally binding decisions, and the terms say AI outputs may be incomplete or out of date and should be reviewed before you rely on them. A match is a reason to look, not a conclusion.

It is also optional. AI features in Linkist are switched on with a separate consent, and you can switch them off in Privacy Settings or by writing to privacy@linkist.ai. AI enrichment outputs are kept for up to 6 months or until you delete your account.
